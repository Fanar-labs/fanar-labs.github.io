Twelve illustrators of dry humour

collection

Illustrators whose characters keep a straight face while the picture is funny.

Deadpan minimalAlmost no detail: flat colour, dot eyes, often no mouth. The joke is in a glance or a pause.
Jon KlassenCanada, b. 1981

Eyes with the pupils pushed to one side, no mouth: the joke is a side glance.

Cards and prints, Eric Carle Museum shop ↗ Cover of I Want My Hat Back, 2011 · © Jon Klassen
Tom GauldScotland, b. 1976

A face of two dots and an eyebrow line, a cream background, two or three muted colours. Many jokes about books.

Portfolio ↗ Illustration from tomgauld.com · © Tom Gauld
NoritakeJapan

One even line, solid black fills, dot eyes. Author of Yomanya, the reading cat of the publisher Shueisha Bunko.

Yomanya on noritake.org ↗ Yomanya for Shueisha Bunko · © Noritake
Scratchy satirical penA fast, uneven pen line with ink blots. Colour runs past the outline.
Ronald SearleUK, 1920–2011

A scratchy pen line that changes thickness, stray ink blots. Fat, smug cats and school satire.

Cats, tribute blog ↗ Cat drawing, scan from the Searle tribute blog · © the Ronald Searle estate
Quentin BlakeUK, b. 1932

A quick, shaky line and watercolour over the edges. Character shows in the pose of the whole body.

Gallery ↗ Musicians in March · © Quentin Blake
Tomi UngererFrance, 1931–2019

Black figures with only the eyes showing, one red accent. The frightening ones often turn out kind.

Children's books ↗ Cover of The Three Robbers, Diogenes · © the Ungerer estate
Newspaper stripA wobbly, lively outline, a background of a few strokes, lettering by hand.
George HerrimanUSA, 1880–1944

Krazy Kat: a nervous line, a desert in a few strokes, lettering by hand. Ignatz the mouse is the spiteful one.

Strips on Wikipedia ↗ Krazy Kat, 1922 · public domain
Gary LarsonGary Larson asks people not to repost his cartoons. They are on his site.
Gary LarsonUSA, b. 1950

The Far Side: animals with stone faces in absurd scenes, one panel and one dry line under it.

The Far Side ↗ Gary Larson asks people not to repost his cartoons. They are on his site.
Tony MillionaireUSA, b. 1956

Maakies: old newspaper pen hatching and Drinky Crow, a cynic with heavy eyelids. For adults.

Galleries ↗ Drinky Crow and Friends, from maakies.com · © Tony Millionaire
Dark elegant witCalm faces and fine hatching instead of flat fill. The humour comes from a pose and a single line of text.
Edward GoreyUSA, 1925–2000

Calm faces in the middle of disaster, blacks made of fine cross-hatching.

Wikipedia ↗ Cover of The Gashlycrumb Tinies, 1963 · © the Edward Gorey Charitable Trust
Wilhelm BuschGermany, 1832–1908

Picture stories: a black silhouette is read through its pose. Hans Huckebein, a raven with a bad temper.

Hans Huckebein on Wikipedia ↗ Hans Huckebein, 1867 · public domain
Joshua BarkmanCanada, comic since 2011

False Knees: nearly realistic birds in watercolour and ink, dry dialogue.
