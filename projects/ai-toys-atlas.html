5scandals over children's toys: four about recording the voice, one about what a cloud model said.Furby was banned at the NSA (1999), though the maker denied it recorded anything. Hello Barbie was hacked (2015–16). Cayla was banned in Germany as a hidden spying device (2017). If speech is recognised on the chip and the toy picks from a closed set of reactions, the voice goes nowhere, no log is kept, and there is nothing forbidden to say ◆.
6 of 9closest relatives of a toy on a chip have no neural network. The effects measured on people came without one too.The nine: Moflin, Keepon, Eilik, Tamagotchi, Furby, Qoobo, Joy for All, Pocket Pikachu, Zoomer — brain inside, few sensors, slow time. No network: Eilik, Tamagotchi, Furby, Joy for All, Pocket Pikachu ◆, Zoomer ◆; for Moflin, Keepon and Qoobo the sources don't say. Measured: Joy for All helped 46 of 54 care-home residents (University of Plymouth); the scripted Hyodol cut depression by 19% in six months. So a network on the chip is an addition to the frame — recognising a voice, telling a stroke from a tap ◆.

Paro is leased to facilities, Joy for All is given out by Medicare Advantage and Medicaid programmes, Hyodol is paid for by municipalities and since 2026 is on Korea's list of care aids. Almost all measured effects in the atlas come from here — and the first measured downside: the longer people live with Hyodol, the smaller their circle of human contact ◆.

Roots

Ideas and experiments that the toys repeat in one way or another.

Braitenberg vehicles1984 · Valentino Braitenberg, the book Vehicles

A cart, two light sensors, two motors. Wires crossed: it rushes at the light. Wires straight: it turns away. There is no thought inside, but an observer sees character.

BEAM roboticsearly 1990s · Mark Tilden

Robots with no processor: a few transistors wired as nerves, often a solar cell. They crawl towards light and "search". Tilden later designed Robosapien (2004).

Subsumption architecture1986 · Rodney Brooks, MIT

No model of the world: layers of simple behaviours — don't bump, wander, head for a goal. A higher layer overrides a lower one. Brooks co-founded iRobot, the maker of Roomba.

Kismetlate 1990s · Cynthia Breazeal, MIT

A robot head with a face and babble: speech-like sounds without words. People talked to it like a baby. Breazeal later founded Jibo.

The Media Equation1996 · Byron Reeves and Clifford Nass

Stanford experiments: people are polite to computers, take offence at them and flatter them, even knowing they are machines.

Uncanny valley1970 · Masahiro Mori

The more a robot looks like a person, the more likeable it is, up to a point. An almost-human feels creepy.

Pet Rock1975 · Gary Dahl, an ad man (USA) · $3.95alive
Body
Senses nothing and responds with nothing. A stone in a cardboard carrier box with air holes and wood shavings.
Brain
No electronics. The "intelligence" is a care and training manual; the most popular trick is "play dead".
Relationship
Presence and a joke; a gift for adults ◆.
Illusion of life
Pet-shop packaging. A manual written with a straight face. Tricks where doing nothing counts as success. The owner supplies the name and the care ◆.
Fate
Shown in August 1975. 1.3–1.5 million sold in a few months; cost under a cent, 95 cents profit per rock. The fad was over after Christmas 1975. Super Impulse bought the rights in 2022; a licensed A24 version came out in 2023.
For a toy on a chip
The idea of a pet was tested on people without a single chip, with packaging and text. The joke lasted months; a chip has to add what a rock lacks: a response to what the child does.

Teddy Ruxpin1985 · Worlds of Wonder (USA) · $59–79alive
Body
The sources name no sensors □. Responds with speech from a speaker and with eyes and mouth: three servos in early models, then two, then one. Comes with books.
Brain
No brain. A stereo cassette: one track carries the sound, the other carries the signals that move the face. Everything is recorded in advance; the bear hears nothing.
Relationship
Storytelling: the bear "reads" aloud ◆. About 60 book-and-tape titles at $12.95; a 65-episode cartoon in 1987.
Illusion of life
Mouth and eyes move in time with the speech. Yawns and giggles while reading. Its creator, Ken Forsse, had built animatronics for Disney.
Fate
41,000 units in the first 30 days, $93 m in sales in the first year, over 8 million in all. Worlds of Wonder went bankrupt in 1987–1988. Then licences: Hasbro, Yes! Entertainment, BackPack Toys, Wicked Cool Toys (2017, stories in an app). A film with Amazon MGM was in the works as of 2025.
For a toy on a chip
Voice and face in sync and good voice acting made it seem alive. The story medium changed four times in 30 years: tape, cartridges, digital cartridges, app. Stories stored on the chip outlive the medium.

Sources: Teddy Ruxpin — Wikipedia · A Bear Necessity — Mental Floss

Toby Terrier1993 · Tiger Electronics (USA) · price not foundsilent
Body
A sensor in the collar watches the TV picture; one button. Responds by nodding, wagging its tail, barking and saying phrases. No screen.
Brain
On the device, no network. The script comes from the tape: special videos hide a data stream in the brightness of the picture (VEIL), and the dog plays matching lines from its memory. Microcontroller □; no neural network ◆.
Relationship
A dog that watches tapes together with the child, ages 3–7.
Illusion of life
The line comes at the moment of the scene. The body answers: nod, bark, tail. Without the right tape it stays silent.
Fate
Sales and end of production □. VEIL was later used in Mattel's Batman toys (2004) and proposed for copy protection (2005).
For a toy on a chip
Behaviour can live in the content the toy watches: the chip only receives a tag and plays a line. A relative of Teddy Ruxpin's control track; the new part is the shared screen — the toy watches together with the child. Without its own content it is silent.

Tamagotchi1996 · Bandai · ¥1,980alive
Body
Three buttons, no sensors. A 32×16-dot black-and-white screen and a beeper. An egg 53 mm tall on two LR44 cells.
Brain
Entirely on the device, no network: an Epson E0C6S46 microcontroller. A table of rules and timers: hunger, happiness and training meters, illness, death from neglect or old age. No neural network.
Relationship
Caring. Children and teenagers in the 1990s; according to the company (2020), about 40% of buyers are adults.
Illusion of life
Beeps when it needs something. No pause: about 12 days from egg to adult. Death is final; children held funerals.
Fate
400,000 in 1996, 10 m by July 1997, about 40 m by spring 1998. In 1999 Bandai wrote off 2.5 m unsold units. Came back in 2004, 2017 and 2023 (with Wi-Fi). 98.1 m by 2025; the brand is alive.
For a toy on a chip
Attachment came from a schedule, irreversibility and requests for attention. A small network first needs the same simple frame of needs; better reactions come later.

Sources: Tamagotchi — Wikipedia · Tamagotchi guide — JapanToPlay · Tamagotchi — The Strong National Museum of Play · たまごっち — Wikipedia (ja) · TamaLIB — GitHub

Digital Monster1997 · Bandai × WiZ · price not foundalive
Body
Three buttons; metal contacts to connect to another toy. A monochrome LCD with eight icons around it, a beeper. 56×41×28 mm, two LR44 cells.
Brain
On the device, no network: a 4-bit microcontroller ◆. Counters and timers: hunger, strength, weight, a "care mistake" if a call goes unanswered for 20 minutes, win rate. The evolution branch depends on the number of care mistakes. No neural network ◆.
Relationship
Care, training and battles with another owner's pet; for boys 8 and up.
Illusion of life
A "call" icon lights up when it needs something. Real time: eight hours of sleep restore strength. Battles through contacts; injury is possible even after a win.
Fate
Japan, June 1997; North America, December 1997. Over 8 million worldwide (Bandai, 2017) or 14 million by 2004 (Anime News Network). The brand is alive: the Pendulum series, reissues in 2017 and 2020.
For a toy on a chip
Built like Tamagotchi. The new part: care turns into "my pet is stronger than yours"; two toys meet without a network, and the creature's shape reads as a record of how it was cared for. For an ESP32: toy-to-toy contact without a router or cloud.

Furby1998 · Tiger Electronics (Hasbro since 2005) · about $35alive
Body
An infrared port between the eyes to talk to other Furbies; touch, tilt and light sensors in early models (the exact list for the 1998 model is unclear ◆). Responds with a speaker and one motor that moves the eyes, mouth and ears.
Brain
On the device: a Sunplus SPC81A microcontroller with 128 bytes of RAM. A script: it starts in made-up "Furbish" and uses more English words over time. The "learning" is written into the program in advance.
Relationship
Play and presence: talks, sings, "grows up". Children.
Illusion of life
A language of its own. "Growing up" on a timer. No off switch: it goes quiet only when the batteries come out. Furbies talk to each other over infrared.
Fate
1.8 m in 1998, 14 m in 1999; over 58 m according to Hasbro. In 1999 the NSA banned Furby from its premises for fear it would record secrets; the maker denied it recorded anything. New versions in 2005, 2012, 2013, 2016 and 2023. The brand is alive.
For a toy on a chip
A cult toy ran on 128 bytes, and its "learning" was a timer. On a chip the main work is planning how the character changes over time; recognition can stay simple.

Pocket Pikachu1998 · Nintendo · ¥2,500 / $19.95silent
Body
Counts steps with a mechanical switch, not an accelerometer: lying flat it counts nothing, so it has to hang upright on a belt. Feels shaking. Monochrome LCD and sound; 40 g, one CR2032.
Brain
On the device, no network. A clock, step counters and a schedule: steps turn into "watts", friendship has levels, sleep from 8 pm to 8 am, new gestures at 25,000, 50,000 and 300,000 steps. No neural network ◆.
Relationship
A walking companion on the belt; no feeding or cleaning.
Illusion of life
Where Pikachu stands on the screen shows friendship: turned away, looking for you, smiling at the edge. Wake him and friendship drops. Ignored, he disappears; a few dozen shakes bring him back.
Fate
Over 2.5 million in the first 30 days in Japan (German Wikipedia citing SFGate, not opened ◆). Followed by a colour version with an infrared link to the games (1999) and Pokéwalker (2009).
For a toy on a chip
New compared with Tamagotchi: the pet's food is the owner's own movement; no chores, but attention is measured; neglect can be undone. The cheapest sensor created its own ritual — wearing it upright on a belt. A natural place for a small network on a chip: an accelerometer telling walking, running, shaking and lying still apart.

AIBO1999 / 2018 · Sony · ¥250,000 / ¥198,000 + subscriptionalive
Body
1999: touch, camera, rangefinder, microphone, accelerometer. aibo 2018: two cameras, four microphones, touch sensors on the head, chin and back, 22 axes of movement, two OLED screens for eyes.
Brain
1999: on the device — a 64-bit 50 MHz processor, software on a Memory Stick, over 100 voice commands. 2018: a Snapdragon 820 on board plus the cloud; it needs a subscription and a constant connection.
Relationship
Presence and play, then care: a pet dog. Adults and high-income families ◆.
Illusion of life
Behaviour "complex and unexpected enough that people don't lose interest" — the goal of its developer, Masahiro Fujita. Adapts to its owner and learns tricks. Since 2018 it recognises up to 100 faces.
Fate
First batch of 5,000; the 3,000 for Japan sold out in 20 minutes. About 150,000 before the line closed in 2006. In 2014 Sony stopped repairs; owners in Japan held funerals for their dogs. The new aibo has been sold in Japan since 2018: 11,111 units in the first quarter.
For a toy on a chip
Its liveliness rested on dozens of axes and sensors at the price of a used car, and the cloud brought a subscription and dependence on the maker. A simple toy needs a self-contained chip and no subscription.

Tekno2000 · Toy Quest (Los Angeles) · $39.99silent
Body
Touch on the nose and head, light, sound, a mode button ◆. Walks forward on four legs only; stops at an obstacle and whines ◆. Glowing eyes, barks and a few English words. 22×29.5×11 cm, with a TEKNO bone.
Brain
On the device, no network; microcontroller □. The manual promises "the intelligence of an 8-week-old puppy"; in practice, simple programmed behaviours and an internal clock ◆. No neural network ◆.
Relationship
A pet and "almost a dog" for children.
Illusion of life
Needs — sources disagree: "required feeding and walks" (V&A) or "only new batteries". A body clock wakes it in the morning. "Gets smarter with age" is a claim ◆.
Fate
"Over 7 million in the first season, over 40 million in 4 years" — Wikipedia citing the LA Times, not opened ◆. The maker's revenue grew from about $40 m to $180 m with Tekno. The original was made until the early 2010s.
For a toy on a chip
The market bought a four-legged puppy for $40 with touch, light, sound and a clock. The "8-week-old puppy" frame forgives clumsiness in advance, as with Pleo and Paro. The new part: a minimal set of cheap senses is enough for attachment; the expensive, fragile part is geared motors. Take the senses, leave the legs.

My Real Baby2000 · iRobot + Hasbro · just under $100silent
Body
Feels motion, light and touch: tickle its feet and it giggles; rock it on your knees and the crying stops. No microphone. A rubber face with seven groups of "muscles" on one motor; speech grows from words to phrases. 46 cm, 1.4 kg with six AA batteries.
Brain
On the device, no network ◆; chip □. A set of states: the advertised "15 human-like emotions" are four levels of laughter and three of excitement. No neural network ◆.
Relationship
Care: a baby doll.
Illusion of life
A bodily care routine: rock, bounce on the knee, tickle — as with a real baby. Expressions from seven muscle groups. Speech grows up over time.
Fate
Work began in 1997 with the Bit prototype; one toy company turned it down, Hasbro took it. "Not a big commercial success" (IEEE Spectrum); withdrawn after "a simpler and cheaper competitor" pushed it out (Gigazine).
For a toy on a chip
Hasbro kept one motor and three sensors because "the face matters more", and it still cost about $100. Complexity that neither the child nor the shopper can see does not sell: whatever brings a simple toy to life has to work in the first minute in someone's hands. Care is given by body movement, not a "feed" button — a place for a small network: telling rocking, tickling, shaking and rest apart from an accelerometer.

For a toy on a chip
Weakness is a requirement: it doesn't walk, it babbles, it may ignore you; the LLM is wanted to leave "room for imagination", not for accuracy. For a chip, the network's goal is believable vagueness; the visible repertoire — tail, sleep, "thinking", babble — fits a table of moods with randomness, and four Cortex-A53 cores are more than that needs.
