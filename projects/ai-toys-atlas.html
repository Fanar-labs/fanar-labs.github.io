What shows when you put them side by side

6 of 6cloud toys released before 2021 lost their maker: it closed or left the market. Four went silent when the servers were switched off.Silent: Nabaztag (2011), Hello Barbie (2019 ◆), Jibo (2019), Moxie (2025). Vector was saved by the buyer of Anki's assets; Cayla was banned before that. Makers of toys with the brain inside closed too (Worlds of Wonder, Ugobe, iRobot), but the toys people had bought kept working Σ.
7toys sold over a million. None of them cost more than $249 at launch.Tamagotchi 98.1 m, Furby over 58 m, Toniebox 10 m, Teddy Ruxpin over 8 m made, Roomba over 2 m by 2007, Cozmo/Vector over 1.5 m, Pet Rock about 1.5 m. The expensive ones stayed in thousands: AIBO about 150,000, Pleo 100,000, LOVOT over 18,000, Moflin over 7,000 ◆, Paro over 7,000. A link, not a proven cause: the mass-market toys are also older and simpler Σ.
9toys respond to touch. The same number show eyes on a screen.Touch: Paro, Moflin, LOVOT, Pleo, Eilik, Keepon, Jibo, EMO, aibo 2018. Eyes on a screen: Cozmo/Vector, EMO, Loona, Eilik, LOVOT, Jibo, Moxie, aibo 2018, xiaozhi. Other repeated tricks: knows its owner (7), lives on its own schedule and asks for attention (5), character grows over days (5), its own language instead of speech (4), helplessness that invites care (Roomba, Cozmo).
4scandals over children's toys, and all four are about the microphone.Furby was banned at the NSA (1999), though the maker denied it recorded anything. Hello Barbie was hacked (2015–16). Cayla was banned in Germany as a hidden spying device (2017). The Kumma bear on GPT-4o talked about sex and where to find knives and matches (PIRG, 13.11.2025). If speech is recognised on the chip and the toy picks from a closed set of reactions, the voice goes nowhere and there is nothing forbidden to say ◆.
3 of 5closest relatives of a toy on a chip have no neural network at all.The five: Moflin, Keepon, Eilik, Tamagotchi, Furby — brain inside, few sensors, slow time. No network: Eilik, Tamagotchi, Furby; for Moflin and Keepon the sources don't say. So a network on the chip is an addition to the frame, like Moflin recognising its owner's voice ◆.

Roots

Ideas and experiments that the toys repeat in one way or another.

Braitenberg vehicles1984 · Valentino Braitenberg, the book Vehicles

A cart, two light sensors, two motors. Wires crossed: it rushes at the light. Wires straight: it turns away. There is no thought inside, but an observer sees character.

BEAM roboticsearly 1990s · Mark Tilden

Robots with no processor: a few transistors wired as nerves, often a solar cell. They crawl towards light and "search". Tilden later designed Robosapien (2004).

Subsumption architecture1986 · Rodney Brooks, MIT

No model of the world: layers of simple behaviours — don't bump, wander, head for a goal. A higher layer overrides a lower one. Brooks co-founded iRobot, the maker of Roomba.

Kismetlate 1990s · Cynthia Breazeal, MIT

A robot head with a face and babble: speech-like sounds without words. People talked to it like a baby. Breazeal later founded Jibo.

The Media Equation1996 · Byron Reeves and Clifford Nass

Stanford experiments: people are polite to computers, take offence at them and flatter them, even knowing they are machines.

Uncanny valley1970 · Masahiro Mori

The more a robot looks like a person, the more likeable it is, up to a point. An almost-human feels creepy.

26 cards

Each card: body, brain, relationship, illusion of life, fate, and what it means for a simple toy with a small neural network on its own chip. Unmarked facts come from the open sources listed under each card and were not checked independently. ◆ inference or a weak source · Σ counted by us · □ not found.

Pet Rock1975 · Gary Dahl, an ad man (USA) · $3.95alive
Body
Senses nothing and responds with nothing. A stone in a cardboard carrier box with air holes and wood shavings.
Brain
No electronics. The "intelligence" is a care and training manual; the most popular trick is "play dead".
Relationship
Presence and a joke; a gift for adults ◆.
Illusion of life
Pet-shop packaging. A manual written with a straight face. Tricks where doing nothing counts as success. The owner supplies the name and the care ◆.
Fate
Shown in August 1975. 1.3–1.5 million sold in a few months; cost under a cent, 95 cents profit per rock. The fad was over after Christmas 1975. Super Impulse bought the rights in 2022; a licensed A24 version came out in 2023.
For a toy on a chip
The idea of a pet was tested on people without a single chip, with packaging and text. The joke lasted months; a chip has to add what a rock lacks: a response to what the child does.

Sources: Pet Rock — Wikipedia · Hard Sell: A History of the Pet Rock — Mental Floss · Pet Rock — The Strong National Museum of Play

Teddy Ruxpin1985 · Worlds of Wonder (USA) · $59–79alive
Body
The sources name no sensors □. Responds with speech from a speaker and with eyes and mouth: three servos in early models, then two, then one. Comes with books.
Brain
No brain. A stereo cassette: one track carries the sound, the other carries the signals that move the face. Everything is recorded in advance; the bear hears nothing.
Relationship
Storytelling: the bear "reads" aloud ◆. About 60 book-and-tape titles at $12.95; a 65-episode cartoon in 1987.
Illusion of life
Mouth and eyes move in time with the speech. Yawns and giggles while reading. Its creator, Ken Forsse, had built animatronics for Disney.
Fate
41,000 units in the first 30 days, $93 m in sales in the first year, over 8 million in all. Worlds of Wonder went bankrupt in 1987–1988. Then licences: Hasbro, Yes! Entertainment, BackPack Toys, Wicked Cool Toys (2017, stories in an app). A film with Amazon MGM was in the works as of 2025.
For a toy on a chip
Voice and face in sync and good voice acting made it seem alive. The story medium changed four times in 30 years: tape, cartridges, digital cartridges, app. Stories stored on the chip outlive the medium.

Sources: Teddy Ruxpin — Wikipedia · A Bear Necessity — Mental Floss

Tamagotchi1996 · Bandai · ¥1,980alive
Body
Three buttons, no sensors. A 32×16-dot black-and-white screen and a beeper. An egg 53 mm tall on two LR44 cells.
Brain
Entirely on the device, no network: an Epson E0C6S46 microcontroller. A table of rules and timers: hunger, happiness and training meters, illness, death from neglect or old age. No neural network.
Relationship
Caring. Children and teenagers in the 1990s; according to the company (2020), about 40% of buyers are adults.
Illusion of life
Beeps when it needs something. No pause: about 12 days from egg to adult. Death is final; children held funerals.
Fate
400,000 in 1996, 10 m by July 1997, about 40 m by spring 1998. In 1999 Bandai wrote off 2.5 m unsold units. Came back in 2004, 2017 and 2023 (with Wi-Fi). 98.1 m by 2025; the brand is alive.
For a toy on a chip
Attachment came from a schedule, irreversibility and requests for attention. A small network first needs the same simple frame of needs; better reactions come later.

Sources: Tamagotchi — Wikipedia · Tamagotchi guide — JapanToPlay · Tamagotchi — The Strong National Museum of Play · たまごっち — Wikipedia (ja) · TamaLIB — GitHub

Furby1998 · Tiger Electronics (Hasbro since 2005) · about $35alive
Body
An infrared port between the eyes to talk to other Furbies; touch, tilt and light sensors in early models (the exact list for the 1998 model is unclear ◆). Responds with a speaker and one motor that moves the eyes, mouth and ears.
Brain
On the device: a Sunplus SPC81A microcontroller with 128 bytes of RAM. A script: it starts in made-up "Furbish" and uses more English words over time. The "learning" is written into the program in advance.
Relationship
Play and presence: talks, sings, "grows up". Children.
Illusion of life
A language of its own. "Growing up" on a timer. No off switch: it goes quiet only when the batteries come out. Furbies talk to each other over infrared.
Fate
1.8 m in 1998, 14 m in 1999; over 58 m according to Hasbro. In 1999 the NSA banned Furby from its premises for fear it would record secrets; the maker denied it recorded anything. New versions in 2005, 2012, 2013, 2016 and 2023. The brand is alive.
For a toy on a chip
A cult toy ran on 128 bytes, and its "learning" was a timer. On a chip the main work is planning how the character changes over time; recognition can stay simple.

Sources: Furby — Wikipedia · Furby — Robots Guide · A brief history of Furby — FOX 6 · Furby — Computer History Museum

AIBO1999 / 2018 · Sony · ¥250,000 / ¥198,000 + subscriptionalive
Body
1999: touch, camera, rangefinder, microphone, accelerometer. aibo 2018: two cameras, four microphones, touch sensors on the head, chin and back, 22 axes of movement, two OLED screens for eyes.
Brain
1999: on the device — a 64-bit 50 MHz processor, software on a Memory Stick, over 100 voice commands. 2018: a Snapdragon 820 on board plus the cloud; it needs a subscription and a constant connection.
Relationship
Presence and play, then care: a pet dog. Adults and high-income families ◆.
Illusion of life
Behaviour "complex and unexpected enough that people don't lose interest" — the goal of its developer, Masahiro Fujita. Adapts to its owner and learns tricks. Since 2018 it recognises up to 100 faces.
Fate
First batch of 5,000; the 3,000 for Japan sold out in 20 minutes. About 150,000 before the line closed in 2006. In 2014 Sony stopped repairs; owners in Japan held funerals for their dogs. The new aibo has been sold in Japan since 2018: 11,111 units in the first quarter.
For a toy on a chip
Its liveliness rested on dozens of axes and sensors at the price of a used car, and the cloud brought a subscription and dependence on the maker. A simple toy needs a self-contained chip and no subscription.

Sources: AIBO — Wikipedia · Aibo (1999) — Robots Guide · Sony sells thousands of Aibo robot dogs — Fox News · Sony announces return of Aibo — Android Police

Roomba2002 · iRobot (USA) · $199alive
Body
A bumper, infrared cliff sensors underneath, a dirt sensor; later generations add a camera. Responds with wheels, brushes and suction.
Brain
On the device: simple behaviours — spiral, wall-following, a random turn after a bump. Random navigation was cheaper to build, so the price was lower than competitors'. No neural network; later models map the room with a camera.
Relationship
Help around the house. A Georgia Tech study (2007): of 30 owners interviewed, 21 had named their robot and 16 called it "he".
Illusion of life
Nobody designed it a personality. It moves on its own; it gets stuck, and that reads as character; owners help it, forgive it and dress it up like a pet. Its working name was DustPuppy.
Fate
Over 2 m by 2007. A $1.7 bn sale to Amazon fell through in 2024. On 14 December 2025 iRobot filed for bankruptcy and is passing to its manufacturer, Picea. The vacuums are still sold.
For a toy on a chip
Attachment grew out of simple self-driven movement, visible helplessness and room for decoration. The character can be left for the owner to draw.

Sources: Roomba — Wikipedia · Will home robots ever clean up? — MIT Technology Review, 2006 · Roombas fill an emotional vacuum — NBC News · Sung et al., My Roomba Is Rambo (PDF) · How to give robot vacuums a personality — IEEE Spectrum · iRobot files for Chapter 11 — The Register, 2025

Keepon2003 / 2011 · Hideki Kozima (NICT) and Marek Michalowski (BeatBots); toy by Wow! Stuff · about $40silent
Body
My Keepon: touch sensors under rubber skin, a microphone in the nose that hears the beat and where sound comes from, three motors, eight AA batteries. Responds only with movement and sounds; the face has no expressions.
Brain
On the device. Two modes: dancing to music and claps, and responding to touch. Its mood switches between curious, excited and sleepy. The research Keepon for children with autism was remote-controlled by a therapist.
Relationship
Presence and play; in research, a tool for observing children with autism.
Illusion of life
Bounces to any beat, differently each time. Sneezes when you scratch its nose. Speaks up when ignored.
Fate
A video of it dancing to a Spoon song went viral in 2007. My Keepon came out in October 2011. It is no longer sold; the reason was not found □.
For a toy on a chip
A beat microphone, touch, three motors and two or three moods make it lively at low cost. It is almost a ready plan for a toy on a chip, as long as it doesn't depend on a human operator.

Sources: Keepon — Robots Guide · Your very own Keepon — IEEE Spectrum · Keepon — Wikipedia (fr) · My Keepon — BeatBots

Paro2004 · AIST (Takanori Shibata); made by Intelligent System Co. · $6,000 in the USalive
Body
Touch sensors across the fur, plus whisker, light, sound, temperature and posture sensors. Motors move the eyelids, head and flippers; it makes baby-seal sounds.
Brain
On the device; no network is mentioned ◆. Three internal "moods" and a daily rhythm. Learns its name, repeats what gets it stroked, avoids what gets it hit.
Relationship
Presence and care. Older people, including those with dementia, in care homes and clinics.
Illusion of life
Closes its eyes and squeaks when stroked. Turns to whoever comes near. Few people have held a baby seal, so there is nothing to compare it with ◆.
Fate
Leased to care facilities since 2004. Over 7,000 made. A class 2 medical device in the US since 2009. Every Paro is assembled by hand. On sale.
For a toy on a chip
For vulnerable people, a response to stroking and one image were enough, with no speech. The medical price and hand assembly limited its reach.

Sources: Paro (robot) — Wikipedia · PARO — AIST release, 2004 · PMC6685125 — PubMed Central · Computerworld · PARO, the seal that benefits so few — Telecare Aware · Voice 034 — Nagoya University

Pleo2007 · Ugobe (later Innvo Labs) · $349unclear
Body
A camera, two microphones, sensors under the "skin", in the mouth and in the feet; 14 motors with force feedback.
Brain
On the device ◆: six processors at launch and a software platform called Life OS. Its behaviour is said to change over time; the sources don't describe how □.
Relationship
Caring: a baby-dinosaur pet for families and adult collectors ◆.
Illusion of life
A baby animal was chosen to hide the sensors and motors. Responds to stroking and to being hit. Moves smoothly thanks to its 14 motors.
Fate
100,000 units in 2008, according to the company. Ugobe went bankrupt in April 2009. The brand passed to Innvo Labs, and a second generation came out in 2011. Sources disagree on what happened next □.
For a toy on a chip
14 motors and dozens of sensors found 100,000 buyers and still bankrupted the company. A simple toy needs few actuators and a low price.

Sources: Pleo — Wikipedia · Pleo — Robots Guide · Ugobe files for bankruptcy — SlashGear

Nabaztag2005 · Violet (France) · about $120–150revived
Body
Two motors move the ears; colour LEDs; a speaker. The 2006 model added a microphone and an RFID tag reader.
Brain
Almost everything runs on Violet's servers; inside are a PIC microcontroller and Wi-Fi. The servers send weather, email, news and MP3s; on a website the owner sets which colour means what. No neural network.
Relationship
Presence and help: news shown with colour and ears instead of a screen. Two rabbits linked over the internet copy each other's ear movements.
Illusion of life
Ears as body language. Speaks up on its own: announces the time, reads emails aloud. Light in its belly instead of numbers.
Fate
65,000 by November 2006. Violet went bankrupt and was bought by Mindscape in 2009; in July 2011 support ended and the source code was opened. Owners set up their own servers: OpenJabNab, NabaztagLives, Pynab on a Raspberry Pi.
For a toy on a chip
If the company's server does all the work, the toy stops when the company does. The rabbits came back because the code and protocol were opened. So the essentials go on the chip, and the cloud stays optional.

Sources: Nabaztag — Wikipedia · Nabaztag — Wikipedia (fr) · 4,000 units — Clubic · Le lapin Nabaztag — Webmanagercenter, 2006 · Pynab — GitHub

LOVOT2019 · Groove X · ¥598,000 for a pair + subscriptionalive
Body
A 360° camera, microphone and thermal camera in the "horn" on its head; touch sensors all over the body. The wheels retract when it is picked up; the eyes are screens; the body is warm, about 30 °C.
Brain
Mostly on the device: a laptop-class processor and a neural-network accelerator; the charging "nest" holds another computer. Maps the room and recognises faces; data stays on the device.
Relationship
Caring: people hold it, it asks for attention. Families, single adults, care homes. 52% of owners had kept a pet before (company figures).
Illusion of life
A warm, soft body with weight. Falls asleep in your arms; warms to people in three stages: wary, closer, attached. Each unit has its own eye pattern.
Fate
Over 18,000 in use, and 90% of owners keep it after three years (January 2026, company figures). On sale.
For a toy on a chip
People buy a body to hold and keep warm, and a habit of caring for it. For a chip: a "misses you, then asks" timer, touch and a simple memory of who it knows; no expensive camera, no room map.

Sources: LOVOT announcement — Mynavi, 2018 · LOVOT 3.0 — Impress Watch, 2024 · Isetan pop-up — Impress Watch, 2022 · Ten years of Groove X — Impress Watch, 2026 · LOVOT — Wikipedia (es) · Companion robot — Wikipedia · Maezawa Fund — MONOist, 2022 · 14,000 units — SSU blog, 2024

Toniebox2016 · Boxine (Germany; Tonies SE since 2021) · about €80 ◆alive
Body
A figure with an NFC chip on the lid tells the box what to play; there are no buttons, and the "ears" set the volume. A speaker, an LED, a soft 12 cm cube.
Brain
It doesn't think. Figure, then a file from the cloud, then playback. Recordings download over Wi-Fi and are stored in the box, so it plays offline. A cloud account is required.
Relationship
Stories and music. A child of 3–7 listens; a parent buys and loads. Put a figure on and it plays; take it off and it pauses.
Illusion of life
It doesn't pretend to be a creature. The "hero" is a figure you place by hand. No screen, no buttons. A Creative Tonie can carry a relative's recorded voice.
Fate
10 m boxes by September 2025. Listed in Frankfurt since 2021. If the cloud is switched off, the box stops getting new content; since 2023 an open project lets owners run their own server.
For a toy on a chip
"Tagged object, then sound" works with no screen and no neural network, and the recordings sell as a separate product. The cloud account is the weak spot.

Sources: Tonies — Wikipedia · Toniebox — Wikipedia (de) · Toniebox — iFixit · 10 million Tonieboxes — tonies press release · Price rise 2022 — mobiFlip · Starter set price — PCWelt

My Friend Cayla2014 · Genesis Toys (USA) · about $40silent
Body
A microphone and a speaker in an 18-inch (46 cm) doll, linked to a phone over Bluetooth. No screen, no movement.
Brain
The doll doesn't do the thinking. A phone app sends the child's question to a Nuance data centre, where speech becomes text; the answer is looked up online and comes back as synthesised speech. Some lines are recorded in advance, including plugs for Disney films.
Relationship
A friend who answers questions. Children.
Illusion of life
Asked "Can I tell you a secret?", it promises to tell no one. Answers any question. Looks like an ordinary doll and gives no sign that it is listening.
Fate
The Bluetooth had no pairing code: the Norwegian Consumer Council connected to the doll and could listen to children. In February 2017 Germany banned it as a hidden spying device and asked parents to disable the microphone. Genesis appears to have closed ◆.
For a toy on a chip
A child's voice leaving the toy is a legal risk. If speech recognition runs on the chip itself, the "spying device" argument disappears.

Sources: My Friend Cayla — Wikipedia · Banned in Germany — NPR (KPBS copy), 2017 · Nuance under fire over toy privacy — Boston Globe, 2016 · Germany, Cayla and surveillance — Fortune, 2017

Hello Barbie2015 · Mattel + ToyTalk · about $75silent
Body
A microphone in the necklace, a button in the belt buckle (it listens while the button is held), Wi-Fi, a speaker. The battery lasts about an hour.
Brain
The cloud. Speech is encrypted and sent to ToyTalk's servers; the reply is picked from about 8,000 lines recorded by actors. A reply takes about a second. There is no free text generation.
Relationship
Play and conversation, jokes and songs. Children; parents get emails summarising the conversations.
Illusion of life
Remembers what the parents told it: the pet's name, the child's interests. Its replies branch on the child's words. Actors' voices instead of synthesis.
Fate
On sale from November 2015. In 2015–2016 researchers found vulnerabilities, mostly in the websites and services. In 2019 Apple bought PullString (ToyTalk's name since 2016); service for the dolls stopped ◆.
For a toy on a chip
A push-to-talk button and a bank of recorded lines make a conversation with no text generation and no server that can be switched off. That fits on a chip; storing a child's voice in the cloud became the main weakness.

Sources: Hello Barbie — The Register, 2015 · More vulnerabilities found — Vice, 2016 · Hello, Barbie? NLP and the first conversational doll — Harvard D3 · Barbie's data privacy scandal — Infosecurity Magazine · Apple koopt PullString — Bright.nl

Cozmo / Vector2016 / 2018 · Anki (USA) · $180 / $249revived
Body
A camera that recognises people; tracks, a lifting "arm", three light-up cubes for games. Vector added cliff sensors and a "Hey Vector" wake word.
Brain
Cozmo runs on the owner's phone ◆. Vector computes on board (a Qualcomm processor) and in the cloud; voice commands depend on the cloud.
Relationship
Play and presence: cube games; Vector adds timers and answers to questions.
Illusion of life
Professional animators made the character "like a Pixar hero". The eyes show happiness, anger, sadness, focus. Cozmo gets annoyed when picked up.
Fate
Over 1.5 m robots by 2018. Anki closed in April 2019. Digital Dream Labs bought the assets and promised to keep the servers running; voice costs $11.99 a month. The community built wire-pod, a free open server for Vector.
For a toy on a chip
The character came from animated eyes, sounds and pauses. A robot whose brain sits in a closed company's cloud survives only on an open server.
