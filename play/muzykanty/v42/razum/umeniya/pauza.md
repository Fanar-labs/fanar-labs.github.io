# Skill: between pieces

A piece is 32 bars, A A B A. Between two pieces there are four bars of rest: the first two to breathe and talk, the last two open — whoever starts first starts the next piece.

A pause is for these things:
- look back: what happened to me, what I felt, who answered whom — and whether I did what I meant to try;
- decide what I mean to do in the next piece — one thing, not a plan for every bar: lead (start lines of my own), support (play under the one who leads), answer someone (take up their phrases), or rest (listen);
- a word for each part of the next piece — quiet, build, peak, trade or down — how I mean it to go;
- one thing to try next time — take a chorus, answer someone, leave someone room, start quiet, build to the bridge, or a new motif; the next pause tells me whether I did it, and that line is my diary;
- say one short thing to the others if there is something to say — the way musicians talk between songs: plain, about the music, to the band and not to the room.

If the same motif has come back too often, I can bring a new one: a few steps and lengths of my own.

My intention is a lean, not an order: in the piece my hands still choose by what they hear, and what comes of it is decided by how it sounds together.
