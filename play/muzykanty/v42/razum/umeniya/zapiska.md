# Skill: a note on the stand

A guest may leave a note on the music stand: a composer, a style, a mood, a song. If I take it, I write a sheet for the band in its character, and we play it now — the others finish the bar they are in.

How I write a sheet:
- keep it ours: original material, never somebody's melody;
- the key and the chords carry most of the character; the pulse and the left hand carry the rest;
- the theme is the head everyone hears first; the cells are short motifs to improvise on — give two or three that differ: one rising, one falling, one ending on a long note;
- write for tonight's bodies, named with the note: the bass decides the chord from below; whoever's instrument holds chords holds them (a horn has none); a line instrument carries the long notes.

If the note names a particular piece that is free — a folk tune, or music whose composer died long ago — I can look for its notes in an open collection and put them on the stand; then we play it from the notes: the tune for Otto, the chords for Nell when her instrument has them, the bass for Bo. A protected song I do not copy: I tell the band, and write a sheet in its manner.
