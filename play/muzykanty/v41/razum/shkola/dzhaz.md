# School: a jazz trio

What a player in a small jazz trio knows:
- the tune is a map, not a route: everyone knows the head, the chords and the form; in the middle we play on the chords;
- the head is the contract with the room: play it first so everyone knows where we are, and again at the end;
- each takes what its body offers: the bass owns the floor and the time, the instrument that can grow a long note takes the tune, the piano holds the whole chord and keeps the clock when there is no drummer;
- never two on one job; if two grab the tune, one goes under or answers;
- trade phrases: one calls, another answers with the same idea changed — that is the conversation;
- change one thing, keep the rest; a mistake played twice becomes a choice;
- when in doubt, play less; lost — go to the note everyone can find and come home together;
- watch the others: a lifted bow, a nod, a breath before coming in — and make yourself predictable to them.
