# Los Angeles, 1953 — The Haig, Wilshire Boulevard
## The postcard
A bungalow club behind a picket fence, facing the Ambassador Hotel across Wilshire ✅. No piano: baritone sax and trumpet weave two quiet lines over walking bass and light drums ✅
## In the window
- Wilshire Boulevard after midnight, red taillights 🔶
- across it the Ambassador Hotel ✅: cream façade, yellow windows, black palms 🔶
- down the street, the hat-shaped Brown Derby ✅, brown, neon-lit 🔶
- the roof sign "The Haig – Dinners – Cocktails" ✅, red neon 🔶
## The room
Gerry Mulligan Quartet: Mulligan (baritone), Chet Baker (trumpet), Carson Smith (bass), Larry Bunker (drums), headlining since Dec. 1952 ✅; players sit in, Lee Konitz in January ✅. Under 100 seats, cramped, mirrors behind the stand ✅. Listening, no dancing 🔶; Down Beat ran an exchange on customer noise, May–June 1953 ✅. On 13 April police took Mulligan and Baker after the last set, near 1:45 a.m., in a full house ✅
## The music here
- taped here 1953: "Bernie's Tune", "My Funny Valentine"; with Konitz "All the Things You Are" ✅
- pulse: light 4/4 swing, ballad to medium ✅
- harmony: ii–V–I standards 🔶; chords implied, not played ✅
- forms: 32-bar songs 🔶, brief double-time ✅
- instruments: baritone, trumpet, bass, small kit; piano stored for Red Norvo's trio, spinet refused ✅. Ours: Otto the baritone, Nell's right hand Chet, her left at rest, Bo walking 🔶; we carry counterpoint and hush, not drum time 🔶
## For our three — the school of this place
- no chords under the horns: two lines answer each other ✅
- the bass alone spells the changes 🔶
- know the chart by heart, as Gerry made Whitlock do ✅
- play to tables a few feet away ✅
- a cello stood here: one May 1953 off night, Oscar Pettiford and Harry Babasin on amplified cellos ✅
