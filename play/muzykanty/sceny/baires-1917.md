# Buenos Aires, 1917 — Café Domínguez, Corrientes 1500
## The postcard
Smoke over marble tables; bandoneón, two violins and piano play two-four; the tables call the next tango. 🔶
## In the window
- Opposite, the Teatro Nuevo: pale stone, doors lit yellow ✅
- Wet cobbles, green tram, yellow lamps, blue-black sky, past midnight 🔶
## The room
- Graciano De Leone's quartet till 1919: his bandoneón, Palavecino and Valotta violins, Vaccaro piano ✅
- Men at tables listen, call tangos till late; no microphones 🔶
## The music here
- Played: El choclo (1903); on 1916–17 discs La cumparsita, El cuzquito, Comme il faut ✅
- Mi noche triste (Castriota's Lita, Contursi's words): Gardel's 1917 Odeon disc, called the birth of the tango-song ✅
- La cumparsita, often dated 1917: Firpo played it at La Giralda, Montevideo, recorded it in 1916, added the third part ✅
- Pulse: 2/4 on the habanera, long-short-two; danced at carnival balls, halls ✅
- Tempo: in 2/4 quarters, estimated 🔶
- Harmony: minor, V7–i, a major part 🔶
- Form: three parts ✅
- Band: flute–violin–guitar trios; Firpo's piano leads, with a second violin (1913); 1917, Canaro's sextet at the Royal Pigall: 2 violins, 2 bandoneóns, piano, bass; Arolas adds cello ✅
- Ours: all three played in 1917 bands; Otto sings the violin–bandoneón line, Nell dry chords on a habanera left hand, Bo bass; no bandoneón breath 🔶
## For our three — the school of this place
- Two-four never stops; the left hand keeps the habanera ✅
- Three parts; the third gets its own colour ✅
- The piano leads and holds the band ✅
- Bass: hit strings with bow or palm, knock the body on beats ✅
- Firpo: unhurried, nuanced; Canaro: fast, shrill ✅
## Free notes from this scene
- El choclo (Villoldo, d. 1919, B minor): IMSLP 1913 prints, ABC; later words not free ✅
- Mi noche triste (Castriota, d. 1932, F minor): IMSLP 1922 prints ✅
- La cumparsita (Matos Rodríguez, d. 1948): IMSLP arrangements, ABC; Si supieras words (Maroni, d. 1957) not free ✅
- El cuzquito (Greco, d. 1924), El entrerriano (Mendizábal, d. 1913): IMSLP; Expósito's words not free 🔶
- Mutopia: none ✅
## The frame
tempo: 66–88 · swing: 0 · loud: 1.1 · hand: stab · bass: oompah · colour: warm · keys: B minor, A minor, F minor
## The style
How a tango goes here — our bodies play it, so a mind can lean on it:
- The pulse is in two: two-four, the strong beat at each half of our bar ✅; the bass and the piano's left hand play the habanera — long, short, two ✅.
- Three parts ✅ along El choclo's route: A (sixteen bars in two), B (eight, played twice), C (sixteen, in the major of the same tonic), and its first ending says «D.C.» — from the start again ✅; we take that as the way of a tango here 🔶. The second part leans toward the relative major ✅; the third is in marcato — a chord on every beat 🔶.
- The bandoneón leads the first part and the violin answers in its gaps 🔶; the second is the song — a voice sings it if a voice is here 🔶; the piano leads the third 🔶.
- It ends with two short chords — the dominant, then home — and silence, the «chan-chan» 🔶 (common in the orchestras of the 1930s and later; for 1917 not confirmed).
- Not here yet: the arrastre (the bass dragged into the beat, 1920s) and the words of a song.
