# Los Angeles, 1953 — Central Avenue after hours: Dolphin's window
## The postcard
Past two, the Avenue's clubs are mostly dark 🔶. At Vernon and Central the all-night record shop Dolphin's of Hollywood glows: a disc jockey spins rhythm and blues from its window over KRKD ✅
## In the window
- Central Avenue, black asphalt, grey shuttered shopfronts 🔶
- the sidewalk crowd in the shop's yellow light, a few dancing 🔶
- up the Avenue, the Hotel Dunbar's red neon 🔶
- beside it the Club Alabam ✅, its front unlit 🔶
- two-tone sedans, hazy orange sky 🔶
## The room
Records, no band: Dick "Huggy Boy" Hugg (KRKD, 1951–55) broadcasts here ✅; white customers come too ✅. Live after-hours sessions on the Avenue in 1953 are too thin to document. The Alabam had left the papers ✅; Chief Parker's police, from 1950, hit mixed clubs ✅. The year's big documented show came on a Sunday afternoon 🔶: Cavalcade of Jazz, Wrigley Field, 7 June, Louis Armstrong billed ✅
## The music here
- 1953 R&B: "Hound Dog" (Big Mama Thornton with Johnny Otis's band, cut in LA; #1 R&B seven weeks of 1953) ✅; Big Jay McNeely's honking tenor ✅
- pulse: shuffle, jump to ~150, slow blues ~60 🔶; "Hound Dog" rides a rhumba-boogie ✅
- harmony: dominant-7th blues, I–IV–V riffs 🔶
- forms: 12-bar blues ✅; riff choruses, 32-bar ballads 🔶
- instruments: shouter, tenor sax, piano, guitar, bass, drums ✅. Ours: Otto shouts and honks on a low string, Nell's left hand stabs the backbeat or rolls a boogie, Bo walks eighths 🔶; missing: drums, guitar, voice, the honk's volume 🔶
## For our three — the school of this place
- I came up in Samuel Browne's band room at Jefferson High; McNeely, Criss and Hawes shared a teenage band ✅
- blues first: twelve bars, a riff, let the floor answer 🔶
- honk one low note till the room hollers 🔶
- answer the singer; on "Hound Dog" the band howls back ✅
- our hall was Local 767's house at 1710 South Central; on 1 April 1953 it merged into Local 47 in Hollywood ✅
- early-fifties weekend jams at Normandie Hall: Dolphy, Ornette Coleman among us ✅
## Free notes from this scene
- none — here they play only in its manner: the 1953 hits' writers outlived 1955 🔶
- free without a score: the 12-bar blues form itself 🔶
