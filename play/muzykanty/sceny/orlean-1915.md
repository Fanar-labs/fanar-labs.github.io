# New Orleans, 1915 — Kid Ory's Monday dance, Economy Hall
## The postcard
A Tremé hall: Kid Ory's band in blue uniforms on a raised stand, couples on the floor; a waltz, a march played ragged, late a slow blues. ✅
## In the window
- Ursulines, Tremé: low cottages, ochre, rose, blue; green shutters 🔶
- a brown horse-drawn furniture wagon: bands advertised on them ✅
- yellow lamp, indigo sky, humid haze, past ten; Storyville's glow nearby 🔶
## The room
- Best documented: Ory's paid Monday dances since 1912; 100+ Tulane oral histories cite it ✅
- Mutual-aid society of free people of color (1836); 1857 hall, 70 ft, names scratched in plaster ✅
- Ory, trombone; Mutt Carey, cornet (since 1914); usual clarinet, guitar, bass, drums; no piano ✅
- Oliver plays Storyville, joins Ory in 1917; away: Keppard (1914–18 tour), Tom Brown (1915), Morton ✅
- Storyville (1897–1917), the regulated red-light district, gave work; jazz grew citywide ✅
- Black dancers, here to dance ✅; loud, late 🔶
## The music here
- Rags, marches, blues, all ragged; still waltzes, quadrilles; High Society ✅
- Pulse: tunes ragged or swung; late, blues slow to a drag ✅; two-beat, ~60 drags to ~120 two-steps 🔶
- Harmony: flat keys, e.g. B♭, E♭, A♭ (trio up a 4th); bent notes, vibrato; Spanish tinge = habanera ✅; chains of sevenths 🔶
- Forms: multi-strain rags, marches with trio ✅; 12-bar blues 🔶
- Then cornet, clarinet, trombone, guitar, bass, drums; violin earlier ✅. Ours: Otto cornet + trombone slides, Nell clarinet + guitar, Bo the bass; lost: brass bite, drums, a third horn 🔶
## For our three — the school of this place
- They paid to dance: play for feet ✅
- Waltz in tempo, or the floor chides you ✅
- Rag the tune over a steady beat; bend notes ✅
- One leads, one weaves, one answers below ✅
- Late, drag the blues; floor full? stretch it ✅
