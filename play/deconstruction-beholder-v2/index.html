Deconstruction — Beholder

from
our deconstruction of Beholder (Warm Lamp Games, 2016)
theme
«the landlord who watches»
goal
narrativechallenge
complicity under pressure: who gets reported is decided by the bill and the deadline, not by conscience
core action
A tenant goes out; I enter their flat with my own key, search it or watch through a camera, find a forbidden thing — then choose: report it on a form (directive + date the law was announced + the thing) for a little money, blackmail for more, help at my own cost, or keep silent. Meanwhile rent and food bills come due every day.

world · 7 — Fullerton’s formal elements
player
single player vs. game — a landlord in a six-flat building of a totalitarian state, watching for the Ministry
objective
pay the bills and keep the family alive while doing the Ministry's tasks
rules
new laws (directives) appear by date and forbid things or activities; a report = directive number + the date the law was announced + a thing in the room; the form is checked, not the truth — planted evidence is accepted like found evidence; evidence belongs to the flat, not the person
resources
money; authority points (they buy cameras — seeing more costs reporting more); time — the bills
conflict
dilemmas — report a neighbour or let your family go hungry; a report pays $250, blackmail $2000, helping costs money
boundaries
one building in cut-away, six flats always in view; tenants move in by event and leave by arrest, departure or death
outcome
the family's fate; an overdue bill means arrest
tests
Does it feel like Beholder? Recognition test — six moments: (1) I enter a tenant's flat while they are away, and it costs me nothing; (2) I find a forbidden thing and must decide; (3) a report pays little, blackmail pays more, helping costs me; (4) the bills tick no matter what happens; (5) the system checks the form, not the truth — planted evidence works the same; (6) to see more I have to report more (cameras cost authority points).
